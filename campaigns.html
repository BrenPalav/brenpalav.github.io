00

Context

A global exchange wanted more futures volume from LATAM, Spain and Portugal. The instrument was a monthly trading campaign with a prize pool: users registered, traded, and the biggest traders won. I owned the mechanics, the prize table, the rules that defined valid volume, and the rollout in Spanish, Portuguese and English. What the business actually needed was not volume on a slide — it was fee-generating volume and new deposits.

01

Prize money went to three people

Share of total trading volume by participant percentile, campaign 1. The top 1% of traders — three people — produced almost half the volume; the top 5% produced nearly all of it. A prize table built around the leaderboard was, in practice, paying the same handful of accounts every week.

Whales trade at lower effective fee rates and rarely deposit new funds. Concentration at this level means the campaign was buying headline volume that barely converted into revenue.

02

The redesign

Campaign 2 kept the leaderboard but added a consistency tier — small equal prizes for anyone trading a modest minimum three days a week — and unlocked the pool in small steps as monthly volume grew, so the next milestone always looked reachable.

Registration to trader

Campaign 2 funnel, as a share of registered users. The consistency tier gave mid-tier users a reason to complete the steps rather than drop out once the leaderboard looked unreachable.

    Deposits are the hard part: this campaign pulled in net deposits worth several times the fee revenue it generated.

    04

    Where the traders were

    Active traders by country, campaign 1. Run in Spanish, Portuguese and English across LATAM plus Spain and Portugal, which is why Brazil and Portugal sit alongside the Spanish-speaking markets.

    05

    What I'd change next

    The consistency tier worked, but the threshold was still set by intuition rather than from the distribution — a lower minimum would likely have pulled more of the middle through without adding much cost. The funnel also shows the real bottleneck is KYC, not trading: fewer than one in ten registered users completed it, and no prize table fixes that. The third campaign should spend its design effort there, and the eligibility window should be defined before launch rather than reconciled afterwards.
