Work

Campaign mechanics: what actually moved the curve

CompletedDashboard
Problem
Futures prize campaigns were paying out to whoever topped the leaderboard. In one campaign the top 1% of traders — three people — produced almost half the volume, so the prize budget kept landing on the same accounts and bought headline volume that barely converted into revenue.
What I did
  • Analysed participant-level results and found the concentration: the top 5% generated nearly all the volume.
  • Redesigned the next campaign around a consistency tier — small equal prizes for trading a modest minimum three days a week — and a prize pool that unlocked in small steps as monthly volume grew.
  • Kept eligibility tied to fee-generating volume only, excluding zero-fee and stablecoin pairs.
Result
On a third of the headline volume, the redesigned campaign produced 41% more fee revenue per dollar traded and moved registered-to-trader conversion from 6.3% to 8.2%.

HIP-3 market share on Hyperliquid, live

In progressDashboard
Problem
There was no public view of how volume splits between the deployers building on Hyperliquid's HIP-3, and the curated tables on Dune sit behind an Enterprise plan.
What I did
  • Wrote a daily logger against Hyperliquid's public API (perpDexs, metaAndAssetCtxs) covering every HIP-3 deployer.
  • Automated it with GitHub Actions and a Google Cloud service account writing to a Sheet, one row per market per day.
  • Built a single-file interactive dashboard (Chart.js) that reads the published sheet, so it updates itself.
Result
Five-plus days of data across all deployers. One deployer holds 94–98% of roughly $3B a day; the rest compete for the margin.
